01 — The case

America was built by people who refused to accept “that can’t be done.”

This movement is for everyone who is tired of being told the future is too expensive, too hard, or somebody else’s job. We believe the country deserves a leader who treats impossible problems as engineering problems — and who answers to results, not to the two-party machine.

  1. I

    Build again

    Factories, power, roads, rockets. A nation that makes things is a nation that leads. We want permits measured in weeks, not decades.

  2. II

    Stop the waste

    Every wasted dollar is a dollar taken from a family that earned it. Government should be lean, transparent and accountable to the people who fund it.

  3. III

    Speak freely

    The answer to bad speech is more speech. A free people can handle open debate — and deserve to.

  4. IV

    Aim higher

    The country that put people on the Moon should never settle for managed decline. We want a future worth being excited about.

  5. V

    Choose freely

    Voters deserve more than two options. If the people want someone on the ballot, the rules should let them have that choice.

02 — The petition

Petition the Congress. Put your name on the record.

To the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled

A Petition for Redress, Under the First Amendment, for a Constitutional Amendment to the Natural-Born Citizen Clause

Exercising the right of the people “to petition the Government for a redress of grievances,” secured by the First Amendment to the Constitution, we, the undersigned, respectfully petition the Congress:

That the Congress, two-thirds of both Houses concurring, propose under Article V a joint resolution amending the Constitution so that a citizen of the United States who has been a citizen for at least twenty years, and who is otherwise qualified, shall not be ineligible to the Office of President by reason of the natural-born citizen requirement of Article II, Section 1, Clause 5; and submit that amendment to the States for ratification.

And we declare our hope that, should the people secure that choice, Elon Musk will stand as a candidate for President of the United States in 2028.

Signing is a public expression of support. It is not a legal filing, a ballot-access petition, or a vote.

Read the proposed amendment text

JOINT RESOLUTION — Proposing an amendment to the Constitution of the United States to make eligible for the Office of President a person who has been a citizen of the United States for twenty years.

Resolved by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled (two-thirds of each House concurring therein), That the following article is proposed as an amendment to the Constitution of the United States:

“Article —

“Section 1. A person who is a citizen of the United States, who has been for twenty years a citizen of the United States, and who is otherwise eligible to the Office of President, is not ineligible to that Office by reason of not being a natural born citizen of the United States.

“Section 2. This article shall not take effect unless it has been ratified as an amendment to the Constitution by the legislatures of three-fourths of the several States not later than seven years from the date of its submission to the States by the Congress.”

Modeled on S.J.Res.15 (108th Congress), the “Equal Opportunity to Govern Amendment,” introduced by Sen. Orrin G. Hatch on July 10, 2003. The age (35) and fourteen-year residency requirements of Article II would remain unchanged.

    03 — How this could actually work

    The honest path from a petition to a ballot.

    We won’t pretend the road is easy. Here’s exactly where things stand, and what would have to happen.

    The obstacle — U.S. Constitution, Article II, Section 1, Clause 5

    “No Person except a natural born Citizen, or a Citizen of the United States, at the time of the Adoption of this Constitution, shall be eligible to the Office of President…”

    Path 1 · The real one · Article V

    A constitutional amendment

    The Constitution has been amended 27 times. Opening the presidency to long-time naturalized citizens would be the 28th. Article V gives two ways to propose it and two ways to ratify it:

    1. Proposal by Congress: a joint resolution passed by two-thirds of the House and two-thirds of the Senate. It does not go to the President for signature. Every one of the 27 amendments began this way.
    2. Or proposal by convention: two-thirds of state legislatures (34 of 50) apply to Congress to call a convention for proposing amendments. This route has never been used.
    3. Ratification by three-fourths of the states — 38 of 50 — through their legislatures or through state ratifying conventions, whichever Congress specifies. Only the 21st Amendment was ratified by conventions.
    4. Time limit: since the 18th Amendment, Congress has usually set a seven-year ratification deadline, in the text or the resolving clause. Without one, ratification can take as long as it takes — the 27th was proposed in 1789 and ratified in 1992.

    It’s been proposed before. On July 10, 2003, Sen. Orrin Hatch introduced the Equal Opportunity to Govern Amendment (S.J.Res.15), making anyone who has been a citizen for 20 years eligible; the Senate Judiciary Committee held a hearing on the idea in October 2004, but it never received a vote. By 2028, Musk will have been a citizen for 26 years. The fastest ratification in history, the 26th Amendment, took 100 days after Congress passed it in 1971.

    Path 2 · The long shot

    A court test

    The Supreme Court has never ruled directly on the full meaning of “natural born Citizen.” A candidacy could test it in court. Be clear-eyed, though: legal scholars broadly agree that the phrase does not include naturalized citizens, so this route is unlikely to succeed on its own.

    Amendments don’t start in Washington. They start when enough people make it impossible to ignore. Every signature here is a public, countable signal to members of Congress, state legislatures and Musk himself that Americans want this choice.

    05 — Questions

    Straight answers.

    Is Elon Musk eligible to run for President?

    Not under current law. Article II requires a “natural born Citizen,” and Musk was born in South Africa and naturalized in 2002. That’s why this petition asks for a constitutional amendment, not just a campaign.

    Is this affiliated with Elon Musk, the America Party, or his companies?

    No. Musk2028 is an independent grassroots movement. It is not affiliated with, authorized by, or endorsed by Elon Musk, the America Party, America PAC, Tesla, SpaceX, X, xAI, or any candidate or campaign.

    Does it cost anything? Will you ask me for money?

    No. We do not collect donations or payments of any kind. If anyone asks you for money in the name of Musk2028, it isn’t us.

    Why is email optional?

    Because a signature is a name, not a mailing list. If you add an email, we use it to confirm your signature is real and, only if you tick the box, to send occasional updates. It is never shown publicly and never sold.

    What does “Verify with X” do?

    It proves a real X account stands behind your signature. You sign in on X’s own site; we receive only your public user ID and username and immediately revoke the access. We can’t post, read your DMs, or see your password. It’s optional.

    What do you show publicly?

    Only your first name, last initial and state (if you gave one), plus an X-verified badge if you verified. Never your email, ZIP or full name.

    How do I remove my signature?

    Right after signing, use “Remove my signature” on this page (the link stays available in that browser). If you gave an email, there’s also a removal link in the confirmation email. See the privacy policy for more.

    Is this a legal filing?

    No. Signing is a public expression of support under the spirit of the First Amendment’s right to petition. It is not a ballot-access petition, a vote, or a filing with any government body. Its power is the public, verifiable count.

    Will this petition be delivered to anyone?

    The goal is to show public demand to members of Congress, state legislators and Elon Musk. The live count on this page is the real count from our database — no padding, no seeded numbers.
